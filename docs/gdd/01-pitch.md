# 1. The pitch

> **In this chapter:** What Squishy Bump is, who it is for and the two ideas that make it different.

## 1.1 Title

Squishy Bump. The name joins the feel of the characters, soft squishy jelly-fruit toys, with the core verb: bumping an opponent off the floor. After closing time in a toy store, six squishy fruits come alive and compete on play mats and toy tables. The short English title is kept identical in both language versions of this document.

## 1.2 Genre

A 3D physics party brawler (ring-out arena game) for one to six players. There is no health bar and no weapon: a fighter is eliminated only by leaving the arena. Competition comes from momentum, mass and positioning rather than from combat statistics.

## 1.3 What players do

Each player picks one of six jelly fruits in the lobby. Its weight class (Heavy, Normal or Light) sets mass, size, speed and traction for the whole session. Players roll, jump and dash their spherical fruit and bump opponents with physically computed impulses. Each of the five levels is a separate arena that adds one living-arena mechanic: a play mat whose outer rings drop away, a reversing turntable inside a counter-rotating ring of obstacle boxes, air and fire cannons around the rim, a slippery ice floor whose tiles crack and fall, and finally all of them together. The ring drops end every Level 1 match, and a 150-second time limit ends every match on the other levels.

## 1.4 Who plays

| Session rule | Single Player: 1 human + 5 AI bots | Multiplayer: 2–6 humans, no bots |
|---|---|---|
| Survival | Advance only by being the last fruit on the floor; an elimination restarts the same level. All five levels in order. | Knockout: after each match the lowest-placed players leave the session until one champion remains. |
| Rank | Three lives per match with respawns; each ring-out scores a point for the last fruit that touched the victim. Points accumulate over the five levels against the five bots. | Same lives, respawns and points; points accumulate between players over the levels chosen by the host. |

In Multiplayer one PC hosts and the others join over LAN or over the Internet through a relay join code. The host chooses the rule and the session length; levels always run in order from L1 to Lk. Single Player always plays all five levels.

## 1.5 Audience and feel

The target audience is players aged 10 and older who enjoy short, social and readable competition, such as party minigames in mobile social games. A match should be understood within seconds: push others off, stay on. Depth comes from reading the arena and playing to the strengths of the chosen weight class.

A match lasts at most 150 seconds and typically 60 to 100 seconds. A full five-level session takes about 8 to 12 minutes including intros and results. The tone is cute and slapstick: jelly fruits wobble, squash and bounce, nobody is hurt, and losing a match is funny rather than punishing.

## 1.6 Unique selling points

Fruit weight classes. The six jelly fruits form three weight classes: Watermelon and Pineapple are Heavy, Apple and Mangosteen are Normal, Peach and Blueberry are Light. The class sets mass, size, top speed and traction, so a Heavy fruit is hard to push but slow, and a Light fruit is quick but flies far when hit. The fruit is picked in the lobby and kept for the whole session, so every player commits to a play style and learns to exploit, or avoid, the other classes.

Living Arena. The arena acts on its own: rings drop, the floor turns and reverses, obstacle boxes ride a counter-rotating ring, pusher blocks cross the floor, cannons rotate and fire, and ice tiles crack and fall. Every hazard is telegraphed and simulated by the host, so it is fair for all players and bots. Each arena favours a different class at a different moment: a Heavy fruit holds its ground against gusts and on the turning floor, while a Light fruit escapes dropping rings and cannon cones in time. This keeps every class viable across the five levels.
